# CONTEXT-MODELS

The models this branch was planned from, kept in the repo beside the work they shaped, so a reader
can see why things are the way they are, and argue with it.

They are **first-person models**: each one speaks from where it stands, instead of describing
itself from outside. A mod says what it costs and what it gives; a reader says what they came for.
That is on purpose. A description from outside tends to lecture; a voice from inside has to make
its own case, and you can tell when the case is weak.

## The layers

Read them in order the first time; after that, go to the one you need.

| layer | folder | who speaks | what it holds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 · ground | `data/` | nobody: measurements | the census (what each mod exposes), sizes, test results, each with the command that made it |
| 1 · ideas | `ideas/` | each mod, in its own first person | what it gives, what it costs (from layer 0), who wants it, where it pulls against the others |
| 2 · readers | `readers/` | each kind of reader the books were written for | what they came for, what they already know, what would make them close the book |
| 3 · contributors | `contributors/` | each person, in their own words | **written by that person and nobody else** (see the rule below) |
| 4 · relations | `RELATIONS.tsv` | nobody: links | every idea linked to every idea it leans on, both ways, with the evidence |
| words | `TAXONOMY.tsv` | the project's vocabulary | each term, where it comes from (the game, Bryant and Alfred's garden, the lab), what it means here |

`PLAN-theseus-argo-disco.md` is the plan for the next layer: keeping these words meaning the same
thing from design to build.

## The rule for people

**No one's first person is written for them.** The contributor files hold a heading, the raw
material that carries the person's name (their commits, their files), and empty space. The person
fills it in, or it stays empty. The one filled file is the instrument's own (`MENTAT.md`), because it
is the only voice here that the writer has the right to use.

The ideas and readers speak in the first person because they are not people: a mod is a piece of
the program, and "the player" is a role any of us can stand in.

## How they were used

- The **ideas** decided which mods could be switched off at all, and how (devguide §3, §8). Co-op
  argued its own case for staying in (`ideas/coop.md`), and won on the numbers.
- The **readers** decided the two books: who each was for, what each could assume.
- The **relations** are the census turned into links; they decided which mods must require which.
- The **words** were sorted so that the game's vocabulary, Bryant and Alfred's, and the lab's could
  live in one file without any of them being renamed to suit another.

## How to check them

`data/` is regenerated by commands (each file's first lines say which). A sentence in a model that
quotes a number must match its data file; if a number here and a number in `data/` disagree, the data
wins, and the sentence is the bug.
